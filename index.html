How I run a lease-up

One process, every building.

A 146-home lease-up and a single downtown condo look nothing alike, but the work follows the same seven steps, and the last one feeds back into the first.

  1. Market & price

    Rental comps, competitor checks and recommended rents before launch.

  2. Launch & list

    Staging, photos, floor plans and listings that answer questions before they're asked.

  3. Manage leads

    Fast, organized follow-up on every inquiry, tracked by source in the CRM.

  4. Tour & close

    In-person and virtual tours, objections answered, follow-up until a decision.

  5. Screen applicants

    Income, references and documents reviewed against each building's criteria.

  6. Sign & move in

    Tenancy agreements, deposits, inspections, keys and fobs.

  7. Report weekly

    Occupancy, pace, funnel and recommended changes, every week.

Leasing intelligence

Every lease-up produces data. I use it.

I track lead sources, tour conversion, applicant patterns, objections, pricing and leasing pace, then turn them into a weekly owner report with clear recommendations.

  • Weekly occupancy and pace against target
  • Conversion from lead to tour to signed lease
  • Lead-source volume and quality
  • Rent achieved by unit type
  • Prospect feedback and objections

What an owner gets

Your building. One point of contact.

  • Leasing strategy

    Market positioning, rental comps and recommended asking rents by unit type.

  • Launch & marketing

    Staging, photos and listings, plus feedback on which channels bring qualified prospects.

  • Lead management

    Fast follow-up, qualification and CRM discipline on every inquiry.

  • Tours & closing

    In-person and virtual tours, follow-up and tour-to-lease conversion tracking.

  • Applicant processing

    Income verification, references and credit-informed screening, including rent-geared-to-income.

  • Leases & move-ins

    Tenancy agreements, deposits, inspections, keys and fobs, coordinated for a smooth first day.

  • Owner reporting

    Weekly occupancy, pace, pricing, objections and recommendations.

  • Building operations

    Resident communication, inspections, maintenance follow-up and suite turnovers.

  • Front desk & concierge

    Resident and visitor service, building access, deliveries and contractor visits.

The goal isn't activity. It's finding what stops units from leasing, then adjusting pricing, marketing and follow-up until the building is stabilized.
